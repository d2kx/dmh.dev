About the Manuscript

What Is the Voynich Manuscript?

Preserved at Yale University’s Beinecke Rare Book and Manuscript Library as MS 408, the Voynich Manuscript is an illustrated codex handwritten on early 15th-century vellum (radiocarbon-dated to 1404–1438 CE).

227 Surviving Folios & 6 Sections

The book spans 227 parchment leaves (~240 pages) and 38,949 words written in an unidentified script by five scribes. Its illustrations divide the codex into six domains: Botanical Herbs, Astronomical Dials, Zodiac Calendars, Balneological (Bathing) Pools, Cosmological Foldouts, and Pharmaceutical Jars & Recipes.

Painted First, Written Second

Across the manuscript, scribes broke their lines 758 times to jump around pre-existing plant stems, roots, and bathing figures. Statistical comparison of words before and after drawing splits confirms the illustrations were painted before the surrounding paragraphs were inscribed.

From Visual Labels to Recipes

Rather than an arbitrary cipher or hoax, short labels written directly onto painted roots (such as oror for blood-red Alkanet root or darary for main taproots), storage jars (okol), and Zodiac star-nymphs reappear verbatim inside the medical recipe paragraphs.

What is a Folio (e.g. f1r, f75v)?

Medieval manuscripts are numbered by parchment leaf (folio) rather than modern page numbers: r stands for recto (front side) and v stands for verso (back side). Multi-panel foldout sheets include a panel number such as f70v2 or f85r2.

What is EVA Transcription?

Because the manuscript uses its own glyph alphabet, scholars transcribe each symbol into Latin letters using the standard European Voynich Alphabet (EVA) (Zandbergen-Landini ZL3b-n corpus). Click any EVA word chip below to analyze its prefix, stem, and suffix.

Currier Languages A & B and Hands 1–5

Prescott Currier identified two distinct spelling and statistical dialects: Currier A (used in most Herbal monographs) and Currier B (used in Balneological, Zodiac, and Starred Recipe pages), written across five distinct scribal hands (Hand 1 to Hand 5).

Grammar, Root Lexicon & Section Atlases

Reference tables showing how Voynich words are built from recurring root stems, how those stems inflect across 8 grammatical roles, and how labels on the Zodiac and Bathing diagrams connect to the recipe text.

1. Root Stems & Physical Grounding

Voynich words are built around a core root stem (such as ke/kee for boiling, ch for herbal infusion, oror for red Alkanet root, or kol for a storage vessel). We ground each stem by checking what physical objects it labels in the manuscript drawings.

2. The 8-Case Inflectional Matrix

Just like Latin or German, a single Voynich root stem changes its prefix and suffix depending on its role in a sentence: o- marks a named ingredient, d-/s- marks taking from a source, qo- + -edy marks an active boiling or washing step, and -aiin / -am marks a measured dose or recipe stop.

3. Zodiac & Bathing Label Polarity

Labels written directly onto painted Zodiac star-nymphs and Balneological water pipes overwhelmingly use nominal o-/ol- ingredient and vessel forms (olkol, okoldy, otar), whereas active qo- boiling commands (qokeedy) appear in the surrounding recipe paragraphs.
